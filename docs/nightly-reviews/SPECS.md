## Program rules — these are recovered, not reconstructed

From [`README.md`](README.md), owner-approved July 24, 2026:

1. **Kickoff-triggered, never scheduled blind.** A night runs when the owner says
   "run night N". The calendar is elastic — skip, repeat, or pause freely.
2. **Every finding is deduplicated** against the open packet backlog **and** every prior
   night before it is filed.
3. **Golden hashes are never regenerated by a nightly run.** A finding whose fix would
   require regeneration is filed as a finding, not fixed.
4. **Enhancement-sized proposals always wait for explicit owner approval.**
5. **Clear-cut fixes ship as normal Codex packets** through the standing review-and-merge
   loop — a night produces ready-to-fire tasks, not direct commits to source.

**Method, common to every night:** automated scans gather measured evidence *first*, then
2–3 focused review lenses adjudicate that evidence, every substantive finding is
adversarially verified, and the night publishes a summary page here plus the fix tasks.
**Findings budget is 10 per night and is a ceiling, not a target** — Night 1 compressed 114
deduped probe findings to 5 filed ones and recorded that it did not pad.

**Co-run rules** are in `README.md` and are recovered. Nights 6 and 8 are pure-static and
pair with anything except 10; Night 7 pairs with any scan night or 6/8; scan nights 1, 2
and 5 each pair with one static night; **Night 3 requires 1 and 2 complete** because it
consumes their scan data; Night 4 pairs with a static night only; **Night 9 pairs only with
6/8** because its timing numbers need a quiet server and CPU; **Night 10 runs solo and
last.**

**Coverage honesty is mandatory.** Every night page ends with a section recording what the
run did *not* cover, any tooling that failed, and any finding verified by a weaker method
than intended. Night 1's page is the model: it disclosed that its first full scan returned
zero findings *vacuously* because every page was a Vite error overlay, and that a verifier
agent crashed and was backfilled inline. A night that reports only successes is not
finished.

# Night 2 — Tablet & awkward middles (768–1024 + 600–900)

**Pairs with:** one static night (6 or 8). May stage back-to-back with Night 1.
**Blocks:** Night 3 (which consumes Night 1 + Night 2 scan data).

**Why this night exists.** The app's breakpoints were designed for phones and desktops.
The 600–900px band is where a layout has too much width to stack and too little to sit
side by side, and it is the band a tablet in portrait actually occupies. Nothing in the
completed nights has measured it.

**Scan phase.** Run the layout scanner across all routes at **768, 834, 1024** (tablet
landscape/portrait real devices) **and 600, 720, 900** (the awkward middles), against the
**seeded e2e server** so tables, cards and pipelines carry populated data — an unmigrated
or empty dev DB scans empty states and returns vacuous passes. Capture the same probe set
Night 1 used: overlap, overflow, touch-target geometry (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8, 24px circle as
the violation threshold), control gaps, and wrap behaviour.

**Lenses [reconstructed]:** (1) *grid and column behaviour* — where does a two-column
arrangement collapse, and does it collapse at a width where it should have held; (2)
*overflow and wrap*, including a visual screenshot pass, since horizontal page scroll is
the most user-visible failure and Night 1 found exactly that class on Testing & launch;
(3) *synthesis and dedup* against Night 1's findings and the open packet backlog.

**Specific to check [reconstructed]:** the arranged dashboard grid now has a curated
full-width span model (DES-11); confirm the `repeat(2, minmax(0,1fr))` tracks and the
`data-page-layout-size` hook behave sanely through the collapse band, and that
`resolveArrangedSpans`' no-holes invariant still reads correctly when a row collapses to
one column. The Overview grid collapses at ≤1180px while Reports collapses at ≤820px —
that asymmetry is pre-existing and intended, so a finding must argue harm, not merely
inconsistency.

**Accept:** every route scanned at all six widths against seeded data; captures retained;
findings deduped against Night 1 and the backlog; each P1/P2 adversarially verified and
re-reproduced before filing; summary page published with a coverage-honesty section.

# Night 3 — Spacing & alignment (1280/1440 + rhythm)

**Requires:** Nights 1 and 2 complete — it consumes their scan data.
**Pairs with:** 6 or 8 only.

**Why this night exists.** Phones and tablets expose breakage; desktop widths expose
*sloppiness* — inconsistent gutters, misaligned card edges, vertical rhythm that drifts
between sections built by different packets. DES-01 and DES-02 normalized tokens; this
night measures whether the normalization actually holds at the widths the owner uses daily.

**Scan phase.** Scanner at **1280 and 1440** across all routes, seeded server. Then a
*rhythm pass* [reconstructed]: extract computed margin/padding/gap values per section and
group them, to find values that are near-misses of a token rather than the token itself
(e.g. a 14px gap where the scale says 12 or 16). Reuse Night 1 and Night 2 captures for
cross-width comparison rather than re-scanning those widths.

**Lenses [reconstructed]:** (1) *token conformance* — which spacing values are off-scale,
and is each one deliberate; (2) *alignment* — card edges, heading baselines, and control
rows across adjacent sections; (3) *synthesis and dedup*.

**Constraint, corrected July 31, 2026 by adversarial audit.** The Overview and Reports
dashboards are byte-pinned by golden hashes over `.metrics-grid`, `.dashboard-grid`,
`.business-kpis`, `.reports-grid` and `.client-directory-banner`. This spec previously said
any spacing finding inside those regions is **file-only** and unfixable without a
regeneration. **That overstated rule 3.** The digests hash `outerHTML` — **markup, not
computed style** — so a fix that changes only a CSS *value* (a gap, a padding, a token
reference in a stylesheet) leaves both constants byte-identical and is perfectly shippable.

What is genuinely blocked is a fix that changes the **markup** inside those containers:
adding or removing an element, changing a class attribute, reordering children. Those move
the digest and would require a regeneration this program never performs.

So the real test is *"does the fix touch markup or only CSS?"* — not *"is it inside a
hashed region?"* **Verify by running `tests/e2e/page-layouts.spec.ts` rather than
by reasoning about it**, exactly as DES-11 did.

**Accept:** off-scale values enumerated with their computed values and sources; each
finding states whether the fix is reachable without golden regeneration; deduped; verified;
published with coverage honesty.

# Night 4 — Collapsible cards & disclosure

**Pairs with:** a static night only (6 or 8) — not 1 or 2.
**Owner-gated:** this night's *spec* was owner-gated in the original program, meaning its
scope needs the owner's confirmation at kickoff before it runs. **[reconstructed — the
gate is recorded in the README's night table as "owner-gated spec"; its exact terms are
not recovered. Confirm scope with the owner at kickoff rather than assuming this file.]**

**Why this night exists.** SET-29 through SET-38 built a four-stage collapsible Settings
shell, and SET-38 added collapsible subsections to Stage 3. Disclosure is where state,
focus, and accessibility most often go wrong: a collapsed section can hide a validation
error, strand focus, or lose a user's place across a reload.

**What to check [reconstructed]:** for every collapsible surface — does collapse state
persist where it should and reset where it should; can a validation error be hidden inside
a collapsed section with no outward signal; is focus handled on expand/collapse; are
`aria-expanded` and the accessible name correct and *constant* across state changes; does
keyboard operation match the visual affordance; and is any content unreachable at mobile
widths because a disclosure control is itself off-screen.

**Lenses [reconstructed]:** (1) *state and persistence*; (2) *accessibility of the
disclosure controls*, axe plus manual keyboard traversal; (3) *synthesis and dedup*.

**Accept:** every collapsible surface enumerated and exercised; hidden-error cases tested
explicitly; axe green in both collapsed and expanded states; deduped; verified; published.

# Night 5 — Design-system conformance

**Pairs with:** one static night (6 or 8).

**Why this night exists.** DES-01 through DES-08 established tokens, control normalization,
card grammar, and unified primitives. Packets built after them may have drifted — reaching
for a raw hex value, a bespoke button, or a private metric component instead of the shared
one. This night measures drift against the shipped design system rather than against taste.

**Scan phase [reconstructed].** Static extraction across `app/**/*.css` and `*.module.css`:
every colour literal that is not a `var(--token)`, every border-radius / shadow / font-size
not drawn from the scale, and every control whose height falls below `--target-min`. Then a
component census: surfaces rendering their own card, metric, empty-state or pill instead of
the shared primitive from `app/components/operations/`.

**Lenses [reconstructed]:** (1) *token drift* — raw values that should be tokens; (2)
*primitive drift* — bespoke components duplicating a shared one, which is also an
architecture finding, so dedup carefully against Night 6; (3) *synthesis and dedup*.

**Known-intentional exceptions.** DES-07 unified `KpiMetric` into `Metric`; the empty-state
and pill primitives are the sanctioned shared ones. A surface predating those and not yet
migrated is a *known* gap — check the DES packet statuses before filing it as new.

**Accept:** drift enumerated with file:line and the token it should have used; each finding
distinguishes deliberate exception from drift; deduped against Night 6; verified; published.

# Night 9 — Performance (numbers required)

**Pairs with:** 6 or 8 only. **Never** with a scan night — render-timing numbers need a
quiet server and CPU during measurement.

**Why this night exists.** No night has measured speed, and the app now carries an AI
review queue, a task list with a 200-row cap, dashboards with per-user layouts, and several
routes that fan out to multiple queries.

**The rule that names this night: numbers required.** A finding without a measurement is
not a finding. "Feels slow" is not admissible; "Reports first contentful paint at 1440 is
Xms across N runs, of which Yms is the client-directory query" is.

**Measure [reconstructed]:** cold and warm navigation timing per route against the seeded
server; the count and duration of API calls per route (the browser network log is the
instrument); any route issuing N+1 queries or fetching a capped list it then filters
client-side; payload sizes for the heaviest routes; and time-to-interactive on the two
dashboards, which carry the most markup.

**Specific suspects, already known and worth measuring rather than assuming
[reconstructed]:** the operations health card issues three category queries; every Gmail
request performs a live, non-retryable refresh grant with no cache
(`_route-helpers.ts` / `google-oauth.ts`), which is recorded in the plan as a rate-limit
concern at scale.

**CORRECTED July 31, 2026 by adversarial audit.** This list previously named a third
suspect — *"the task list requests 200 rows and filters client-side"* — which is **false**.
`taskManagementSearch` (`app/assistant/task-management.ts:141-150`) puts `status`,
`assigneeEmail`, `dueBefore` and `projectId` into the query string and the API filters
server-side (`app/api/v1/tasks/route.ts:120`). The 200 is a result cap, not a
fetch-everything-then-filter pattern. Night 9 would have been chartered to measure
something that does not exist. **Verify each remaining suspect against source before
measuring it** — these are reconstructed, and one of three was wrong.

**Accept:** every finding carries a measurement, the method, and the run count; no
finding rests on a single sample; the quiet-machine condition is stated; deduped; verified;
published with coverage honesty naming what was not measured.

# Night 10 — Accessibility + maintenance + synthesis

**Runs solo and last.** It synthesizes every prior night.

**Why this night exists.** It is the program's closing argument: a full accessibility pass,
a maintenance sweep, and a synthesis that turns nine nights of findings into a prioritized
picture rather than nine separate lists.

**Accessibility pass [reconstructed]:** axe across all routes at 1280 and 390 in both
default and editing states; keyboard-only traversal of every mutating flow (create lead,
edit client, edit task, file an email, accept a review row); focus management on every
modal and drawer, including return-focus on close; screen-reader semantics on the surfaces
that carry live state — toasts, the review queue, connection health, and the layout editor;
and colour-contrast verification against the shipped tokens rather than against screenshots.

**Maintenance sweep [reconstructed]:** dependency and lockfile health; skipped or
quarantined tests and why each is skipped; e2e flake census — this repo has known flakes in
`fix15-toast-and-folds` and `postgres-suite-gating`, both of which cost real time today;
docs whose claims have gone stale, checked against source rather than against each other,
which is the failure mode that produced an eleven-day-stale deployment line.

**Synthesis.** Read every prior night page. Produce: what recurs across nights; what was
filed but never fixed; which findings the shipped code has since made obsolete; and the
three things most worth doing next, argued rather than asserted.

**Accept:** axe results per route and state; keyboard traversal recorded per flow; flake
census with counts; a synthesis section that cites prior night pages by ID; deduped;
verified; published with a coverage-honesty section stating what the program as a whole
never examined.
